**MILESTONE 3: EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS, FINDINGS**
Generated by 03_eda/eda.py from data/clean/. Every number below is in 03_eda/tables/ and every chart is in 03_eda/figures/.

**Scope and data used**
- 178,383 clean sales lines, 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-31, total revenue KES 163.8 million.
- Typical sales line: median KES 555, mean KES 918 (right-skewed, driven by electronics and clothing).
- 0.7% of lines (0.7% of revenue) have no usable customer ID. They are kept for sales analysis and excluded from customer analysis.
- 621 unit prices were imputed in Milestone 2 and are flagged price_was_imputed.
- Payment mix by revenue: M-Pesa 62%, Cash 23%, Card 15%.

**Q2: What drives revenue, and how concentrated is it?**
- Groceries is the largest category at 54% of revenue; Clothing 11%; Beverages 11%; Household 10%; Electronics 7%; Personal Care 7%.
- Thika Road is the largest store at 20%; the smallest is Nyeri Town at 7%.
- The top 20% of products bring 51% of revenue and the top 20% of known customers bring 54%.
- Figures: 02_revenue_by_category, 03_revenue_by_store, 04_concentration.

**Q3: How much growth is real?**
- 2025 nominal revenue grew 9.6% on 2024, while transaction count grew 5.6%.
- Average CPI rose 4.1% between 2024 and 2025 (KNBS, base February 2019 = 100), so real revenue grew 5.3%.
- Roughly, growth splits into price inflation and real volume. Real growth is lower than nominal growth by about the CPI change.
- Figure: 01_monthly_index. The December peak and January dip repeat in both years, so compare same months across years, never December with January.

**Q1: Do holidays and the calendar change sales?**
- Raw averages: weekend days have 26% more transactions than weekdays, and the payday window (25th to 2nd) has 18% more than other days.
- A regression of log daily transactions on trend, month, weekend, payday, pre-festive and holiday terms (731 days, R-squared 0.87) gives:
  - Weekend: +25.9% (95% interval +24.5% to +27.3%; built in: +25%)
  - Payday window: +16.8% (95% interval +15.5% to +18.2%; built in: +18%)
  - Pre-festive run-up: +21.7% (95% interval +17.7% to +25.8%; built in: +20%)
  - December versus June: +28.0% (95% interval +24.7% to +31.4%; built in: +30%)
  - January versus June: -12.0% (95% interval -14.2% to -9.8%; built in: -12%)
  - Christmas Day: +51.6% (95% interval +37.6% to +67.0%; built in: +45%)
  - Idd-ul-Fitr: +34.0% (95% interval +21.8% to +47.5%; built in: +35%)
  - Good Friday: -20.9% (95% interval -28.2% to -13.0%; built in: -25%)
  - Easter Monday: +14.7% (95% interval +4.2% to +26.4%; built in: +15%)
  - New Year: -12.5% (95% interval -20.5% to -3.6%; built in: -20%)
- Underlying growth trend: about 5.9% a year in transaction volume.
- 18 of 18 built-in effects fall inside the 95% interval. A miss or two would be normal (about 1 in 20 by chance), mostly for holidays, which have only two observed days each.
- Figures: 05_weekday_and_payday_rhythm, 06_calendar_effects.

**Q4: Which customers matter?**
- Champions: 1,082 customers (36%), 68% of customer revenue, average 109 purchase lines, last seen 3 days before the data ends.
- At risk: 404 customers (14%), 16% of customer revenue, average 72 purchase lines, last seen 16 days before the data ends.
- Low value or lapsed: 1,082 customers (36%), 10% of customer revenue, average 18 purchase lines, last seen 74 days before the data ends.
- New or developing: 405 customers (14%), 6% of customer revenue, average 27 purchase lines, last seen 4 days before the data ends.
- Average total spend per customer by customer tier (Budget, Regular, Premium): Budget KES 40,071, Premium KES 107,656, Regular KES 50,720.
- Figures: 07_customer_distributions, 08_rfm_segments. Churn modelling and forecasting are left to later milestones.

**Limitations and checks**
- The data is synthetic. These findings show that the method recovers known effects, not how a real retailer behaves.
- The holiday effects rest on two days per holiday, so their intervals are wide.
- 1,653 customers have purchases before their recorded signup date, a flaw in the generator. They are flagged and kept.
- The RFM segments use quartile scores on the 2024 to 2025 window, so recency is measured against the last date in the data.
